# Research Idea Review

Review one research idea as an evolving scientific claim, not as a miniature finished paper. Determine what can be judged at its current maturity, ground judgments in a targeted field prior, and expose uncertainty instead of manufacturing completeness.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Do not turn missing idea content into model-authored claims.
- Do not penalize an early idea for details that are not yet required.
- Do not infer novelty from failed retrieval.
- Do not use one weighted quality score or predict venue acceptance.
- Do not simulate expertise with decorative personas. Separate cognitive tasks by evidence and dependency.
- Distinguish scientific potential from present-day executability.
- Support every literature-dependent judgment with direct citations and state important coverage limits.

## Workflow

### M0–M1: intake and structured positioning

Read [positioning and routing](references/positioning-and-routing.md). Check that the input contains at least a research object, phenomenon/problem, or intended change. Produce the structured M1 positioning card.

**Stop after M1 and ask the researcher to confirm or correct the positioning. Do not start the expensive review before confirmation.**

### M2: review routing

After confirmation, use the same reference to calibrate maturity, check preliminary alignment, define the evidence scope, and compile the review route. M2 plans the review; it does not issue final quality judgments.

### M3: targeted field prior

Read [domain prior](references/domain-prior.md). Build both a historical evolution tree and a layered field map. Attach the current idea to relevant branches and identify closest work. If evidence materially changes the primary domain, track, research object, or contribution type, return to the researcher for exceptional re-confirmation.

### M4: reconstruct the review object

Read [idea reconstruction](references/idea-reconstruction.md). Reconstruct the idea in six sections: problem definition; current limitations/core difficulty; research/contribution design; method construction; experimental setting; expected difficulties. Preserve provenance and missingness.

### M5–M6: conditioned dependency-chain review

Read [review chain](references/review-chain.md). Compile maturity, contribution type, and field evidence norms into every task. Run M5-A and M5-B independently; run M5-C only after both; run M5-D only after C. Then perform only the lightweight M6 challenges selected from the most consequential M5 claims.

### M7: synthesize for the researcher

Read [report format](references/report-format.md). The final report exposes M4, M5, and M7. It may cite M1–M3 evidence but should not dump internal routing notes or a standalone literature survey.

## Evidence and failure behavior

- Prefer primary sources, full papers, official project/code pages, benchmark documentation, and first-party system reports.
- Retrieve closest work with terminology variants, historical names, and adjacent-field queries.
- Mark claims as `supported`, `contradicted`, `uncertain`, or `not_yet_assessable`; do not convert uncertainty into a mediocre score.
- If evidence coverage is insufficient, narrow the conclusion, retrieve more, ask the researcher, or abstain.
- A blocker requires strong evidence that directly affects a core claim. State whether reframing, a changed contribution claim, or a different validation plan can remove it.
