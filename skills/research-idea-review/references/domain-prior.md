# Targeted Domain Prior (M3)

M3 builds a decision-oriented related-work model, not an exhaustive survey. It must support the confirmed positioning, M4 reconstruction, and every evidence-dependent M5 judgment.

## Two complementary structures

### Historical evolution tree

Organize the relevant research history around changes in problems, assumptions, and solution paths:

```text
problem origin
├── branch A: problem framing or method family
│   ├── foundational work
│   ├── important transition
│   ├── recent work
│   └── known limitation/open tension
├── branch B: competing path
│   └── ...
└── adjacent-field branch
    └── possible collision or transferable mechanism
```

Attach the current idea to one or more nodes. Explain whether it continues, combines, redirects, or challenges those paths. Add cross-links when data, evaluation, or assumptions connect otherwise separate branches.

### Layered field map

#### Foundation and consensus

- historical development of the domain and task;
- canonical problem definitions and terminology;
- classic works and method families;
- accepted knowledge, recurring assumptions, and established failures;
- active controversies where no consensus exists.

#### Data and research infrastructure

- datasets, benchmarks, populations, environments, and workloads;
- canonical baselines and evaluation protocols;
- metrics and their construct limitations;
- accessible code, systems, models, tools, and other resources;
- contamination, leakage, shortcut, representation, access, and licensing risks.

#### Frontier and practice

- recent papers and preprints that materially affect the idea;
- current experimental attempts and negative results;
- industrial or deployment practice where relevant;
- emerging concerns, bottlenecks, and shifting evaluation norms;
- plausible gaps, with uncertainty stated.

## Retrieval behavior

Search outward from the confirmed problem scenario rather than only from the proposed method name.

Include as needed:

- closest-work search;
- terminology-variation and historical-name search;
- cited/citing lineage search;
- adjacent-field collision search;
- data, benchmark, code, and system documentation;
- known-failure and negative-result search;
- recent frontier and first-party industrial-practice search.

Prefer direct inspection of the work that supports a decisive claim. Abstracts and search snippets may identify candidates but should not carry a strong novelty, mechanism, or blocker conclusion by themselves.

## Evidence record

For each source that affects the review, record:

- source and date;
- branch/layer;
- claim supported or challenged;
- relevance to the current idea;
- evidence scope and limitation.

Distinguish `not found in covered sources` from `shown not to exist`.

## Stopping conditions

Stop expanding M3 when:

- the main historical branches are covered;
- the idea can be attached to the evolution tree;
- closest works and the natural next-step path are identifiable;
- relevant data/evaluation/resource norms are understood;
- current frontier, major disputes, and known failures are represented;
- further retrieval is unlikely to change the review route or a key judgment.

If these conditions cannot be met, record the coverage gap and constrain or abstain from the affected M5 judgment.

## Evidence-triggered repositioning

Minor refinements update the internal route. If strong evidence changes the primary domain, track, research object, or contribution type, present the revised M1 positioning and obtain exceptional researcher confirmation before continuing.
