# Targeted research within stage 2

Research serves a concrete unresolved question. Before searching, identify what decision could change.

- Contribution: discover closest work using problem, mechanism, evaluation, historical terminology and adjacent formulations; inspect decisive original papers.
- Value: inspect whether the phenomenon is real or a benchmark/measurement artifact.
- Mechanism: inspect assumptions, simpler alternatives and evidence that could distinguish explanations.
- Testability: inspect evaluation protocols, data limits and necessary resource access.

Use bibliographic indexes/search for discovery, original papers for substantive claims, and official resources for current access/protocol facts. A snippet is a lead. Do not infer nonexistence from failed retrieval.

Compare closest work by claim, assumptions, method and evaluation boundaries. Inspect the differences that could preserve a legitimate contribution. A direct subsumption decision needs substantive inspected evidence, not theme similarity.

A field map may emerge from this work. Do not build a mandatory evolution tree or cover foundation/data/frontier symmetrically. Broaden only when terminology changes, cross-field equivalences or disputes affect judgment.

Save meaningful research batches with question_id, decision_impact and outcome. Keep source locators and limits short and specific. Store trace logs separately if needed; do not pass them as scientific handoff prose. Stop when the important judgments are supported or explicitly unresolved, not when a literature-map template is full.
